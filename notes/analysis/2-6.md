## Exercise 2.6.4

> [!Question]
> Let $(a_{n})$ and $(b_{n})$ be Cauchy sequences. Determine whether $c_{n} = |a_{n} - b_{n}|$ is a Cauchy sequence.

**Proof.**
Yes. To show that $(c_{n})$ is a Cauchy sequence, we need to show that for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $m, n \geq N$ implies

$$
\big||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}|\big| < \epsilon.
$$

Because $(a_{n})$ and $(b_{n})$ are Cauchy, there exist $N_{a}, N_{b} \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

$$
|a_{n} - a_{m}| < \frac{\epsilon}{2}
\quad \text{and} \quad
|b_{n} - b_{m}| < \frac{\epsilon}{2}
$$

whenever $m, n \geq N_{a}$ and $m, n \geq N_{b}$, respectively. Choose $N = \max\{N_{a}, N_{b}\}$. Then for every $m, n \geq N$, the reverse triangle inequality and the triangle inequality give

$$
\begin{aligned}
|c_{n} - c_{m}|
&= \big||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}|\big| \\
&\leq |(a_{n} - b_{n}) - (a_{m} - b_{m})| \\
&= |(a_{n} - a_{m}) + (b_{m} - b_{n})| \\
&\leq |a_{n} - a_{m}| + |b_{m} - b_{n}| \\
&< \frac{\epsilon}{2} + \frac{\epsilon}{2} = \epsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $(c_{n})$ is a Cauchy sequence. $\blacksquare$
