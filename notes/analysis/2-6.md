## Exercise 2.6.4

> [!Question]
> Let $(a_{n})$ and $(b_{n})$ be Cauchy sequences. Determine whether $c_{n} = |a_{n} - b_{n}|$ is a Cauchy sequence.

**Proof.**
To show $c_{n} = |a_{n} - b_{n}|$ is a Cauchy sequence, we need to show that for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $m, n \geq N$ implies

$$
||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}|| < \epsilon
$$

We use the reverse triangle inequality

$$
\begin{aligned}
||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}||
&\leq |a_{n} - b_{n} - (a_{m} - b_{m})| \\
&\leq |a_{n} - a_{m} - b_{n} + b_{m}| \\
&\leq |(a_{n} - a_{m}) + (b_{m} - b_{n})|
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

Using the triangle inequality

$$
|(a_{n} - a_{m}) + (b_{m} - b_{n})| \leq |a_{n} - a_{m}| + |b_{m} - b_{n}|
\tag{2}
$$

We are given that $(a_{n})$ and $(b_{n})$ are Cauchy sequences. By the definition of Cauchy, there exists an $N_{a}$ and $N_{b}$ where the terms in $(a_{n})$ and $(b_{n})$ get arbitrarily close. We then choose $N = \text{max}\{N_{a}, N_{b}\}$ such that

$$
|a_{n} - a_{m}| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad |b_{m} - b_{n}| < \frac{\epsilon}{2}
$$

for every $m, n \geq N$.

Then we can combine with the LHS of (1) and substitute into the RHS of (2)

$$
||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}|| \leq |(a_{n} - a_{m}) + (b_{m} - b_{n})| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} + \frac{\epsilon}{2} = \epsilon
$$

We have found an $N$ where $||a_{n} - b_{n}| - |a_{m} - b_{m}|| < \epsilon$ for every $m, n \geq N$. That means $c_{n} = |a_{n} - b_{n}|$ is Cauchy. $\blacksquare$
